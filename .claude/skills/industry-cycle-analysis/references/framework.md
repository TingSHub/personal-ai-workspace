# Framework

Use this reference when an industry needs to be decomposed into a cycle rather than merely described.

## Core Lens

The skill studies a system, not a ticker.

```text
real production activity
-> industry-chain transmission
-> supply-demand mismatch
-> capacity response
-> price/order/inventory signal
-> profit redistribution
-> capital-market expectation
```

The goal is to understand how a sector grows, becomes tight, attracts capacity, becomes crowded, clears, and creates the next round of opportunities.

## Three Axes

### 1. Vertical Axis: Industry Evolution

Ask:

- How did this industry appear?
- What demand, technology, policy, or capital condition made it possible?
- What were the last major cycle turns?
- Which historical industry is it similar to: chemical, steel, coal, lithium battery, photovoltaic, semiconductor, optical communication, data center, or another?

### 2. Horizontal Axis: Industry Chain

Map:

- raw materials
- equipment
- components
- manufacturing
- integration
- channels
- end customers
- substitutes
- after-sales or operation layer

For each node, identify:

- what the node is in plain language
- what the node does in the production or service chain
- who supplies it
- who buys it
- main companies
- capacity
- capacity utilization
- customer qualification barriers
- revenue model or monetization method
- pricing power
- margin level
- bottleneck probability

### 3. Depth Axis: Power and Money

Ask:

- Who pays?
- Who captures gross profit?
- Who bears capex risk?
- Who has pricing power?
- Who has scarce capacity?
- Who appears important but cannot monetize?
- Who benefits first and who benefits later?

## Standard Chain Examples

Semiconductor:

```text
silicon/materials
-> equipment
-> wafer fabrication
-> advanced packaging
-> testing
-> chips
-> servers/devices
-> AI/application demand
```

Optical communication:

```text
optical chip
-> optical device
-> optical module
-> switch
-> data center
-> AI computing demand
```

AI data center:

```text
GPU/ASIC
-> HBM
-> advanced packaging
-> server
-> switch/network
-> power
-> cooling
-> data center
-> model/application demand
```

## Required Distinctions

Always separate:

- shortage caused by real demand vs shortage caused by inventory hoarding
- structural shortage vs temporary mismatch
- bottleneck component vs replaceable component
- company with earnings elasticity vs company with only concept exposure
- industry reality vs market expectation
- fact vs inference vs assumption
