# Cycle Stages

Use these stages to classify where an industry is in its lifecycle. The stage is a hypothesis to be updated, not a fixed label.

When a prompt uses capital-cycle shorthand such as `涌入 / 狂热 / 崩盘 / 继承`, load `capital-cycle-diagnostics.md` and map the evidence back to the stages below. The shorthand is not a separate canonical stage system, and capex plus one return proxy cannot identify a stage by itself.

## 1. Introduction

Signals:

- technology route is not stable
- product-market fit is unclear
- few customers can verify value
- capital watches but has not fully entered
- data is sparse and narrative dominates

Common mistake: assuming every new technology will become a large industry.

## 2. Growth

Signals:

- end demand becomes measurable
- customer adoption accelerates
- leading firms establish product advantage
- capacity starts to become important
- financing and capex increase

Common mistake: confusing high growth with high profitability.

## 3. Shortage

Signals:

- delivery time lengthens
- prices rise
- order backlog increases
- utilization stays high
- downstream complains about supply
- margins improve for the constrained node

Common mistake: discovering the shortage only after the market has priced it.

## 4. Expansion

Signals:

- companies announce capex
- equipment orders rise
- factories start construction
- upstream suppliers report strong orders
- local governments and capital enter

Common mistake: treating announced capacity as effective capacity.

## 5. Earnings Realization

Signals:

- revenue and margins improve
- financial statements confirm earlier industry signals
- sell-side coverage becomes dense
- public awareness increases

Common mistake: assuming earnings realization means stock prices must continue rising.

## 6. Oversupply

Signals:

- new capacity releases
- price starts falling
- inventory rises
- utilization drops
- weaker firms cut prices
- capex plans get delayed or canceled

Common mistake: believing a good industry cannot become overbuilt.

## 7. Clearing

Signals:

- high-cost capacity exits
- consolidation increases
- marginal players lose financing
- pricing stabilizes after losses
- survivors gain share

Common mistake: missing the next cycle because the industry looks bad at the bottom.

## Cycle Question

Always answer:

```text
If this industry is right, what stage are we in?
If this stage judgment is wrong, what signal would disprove it?
```
